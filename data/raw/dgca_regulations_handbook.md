# Comprehensive DGCA Drone Regulations Handbook (India)

## 1. Overview of Drone Rules 2021
The Ministry of Civil Aviation notified the liberalized **Drone Rules 2021** on August 25, 2021, replacing the earlier Unmanned Aircraft System (UAS) Rules 2021. The revised framework significantly simplified compliance, reduced total approval forms from 25 to 5, eliminated security clearance requirements before registration, and lowered license fees.

### Key Regulatory Highlights:
- **Coverage**: Applies to all unmanned aircraft systems registered in India or operated within Indian airspace up to an all-inclusive maximum takeoff weight (MTOW) of 500 kg.
- **DigitalSky Platform**: Single-window online portal (digitalsky.dgca.gov.in) for registration, UIN allocation, flight permissions, and airspace map access.
- **Classification by Weight**:
  1. **Nano**: MTOW less than or equal to 250 grams.
  2. **Micro**: MTOW greater than 250 g and less than or equal to 2 kg.
  3. **Small**: MTOW greater than 2 kg and less than or equal to 25 kg.
  4. **Medium**: MTOW greater than 25 kg and less than or equal to 150 kg.
  5. **Large**: MTOW greater than 150 kg.

## 2. Airspace Zoning Framework & Flight Permission Workflow
India's airspace is categorized into three color-coded zones displayed on the interactive DigitalSky map:

### Green Zone:
- Airspace up to a vertical distance of 400 feet (120 meters) above ground level in areas not designated as yellow or red zones.
- Includes airspace up to 200 feet (60 meters) above ground level in areas between 8 km and 12 km from the perimeter of an operational airport.
- **Flight Permission Requirement**: **No prior flight permission** is needed for flying in green zones. Operators only log their flight intent on DigitalSky.

### Yellow Zone:
- Controlled airspace up to 400 feet located between 8 km and 12 km from an operational airport perimeter, or airspace above 400 feet in green zones.
- **Flight Permission Requirement**: Mandatory prior flight permission from Air Traffic Control (ATC) via DigitalSky.

### Red Zone:
- Airspace around vital installations, national defense infrastructure, international borders (within 25 km), and within 5 km of operational international airports.
- **Flight Permission Requirement**: Flying is strictly prohibited except under direct authorization from the Central Government.

## 3. Registration, Certification & Pilot Requirements

### Unique Identification Number (UIN):
Every drone operating in India (except non-commercial nano drones) must possess a valid UIN issued on DigitalSky. Self-certification of serial number and hardware specifications is conducted online.

### Remote Pilot Certificate (RPC):
- To operate a drone commercially in India, pilots must hold an RPC issued by a DGCA-authorized Remote Pilot Training Organization (RPTO).
- **Eligibility**: Minimum age 18 years, maximum age 65 years, passed 10th standard exam or equivalent from a recognized board.
- **Validity**: RPC remains valid for 10 years and can be renewed via refresher training.

### NPNT (No Permission, No Take-off):
Drones must be integrated with NPNT firmware/hardware architecture. Before arming motors, the drone verifies signed permission artifacts from DigitalSky; if unapproved, motors will refuse to spin.

## 4. Import Regulations & Financial Incentives
- **CBU Import Prohibition**: In February 2022, Directorate General of Foreign Trade (DGFT) prohibited the import of completely built-in (CBU) foreign drones to foster Make in India domestic manufacturing.
- **Component Imports**: Import of drone components (flight controllers, motors, carbon fiber frames, sensors, ESCs) remains unrestricted.
- **Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme**: Provides 20% financial incentive on net value addition achieved by Indian manufacturers of drones and drone components over three fiscal years.
